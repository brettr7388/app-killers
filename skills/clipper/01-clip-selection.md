# Picking the moment

The cut is 80% of the result. A perfectly formatted clip of a mediocre moment
still dies. This is the part every auto-clipper tool gets wrong, because volume
spikes and "engagement scores" don't know what a joke is — you're reading the
transcript, so you do.

## What to scan the transcript for

**Reversals.** Setup states one thing, reality delivers the opposite. Someone says
"there's no way he's still alive" — and he is. This is the single highest-performing
pattern and it's findable in text: look for confident declarative statements
followed within 10 seconds by a reaction word.

**Reaction words.** `what`, `wait`, `no way`, `bro`, `stop`, `hello?`, `excuse me`,
laughter, and profanity clusters. These mark the *end* of a moment. The clip starts
5–15 seconds *before* them, at the setup.

**Rapid alternation.** Several short Whisper segments in a row from a back-and-forth
means an argument or a bit. Those travel well.

**Quotables.** A short declarative sentence that stands alone with zero context.
"I've never lost a fight I didn't start." Standalone lines are the only clips that
work with no setup at all, which makes them the shortest and often the best.

**Long pauses.** A gap of 2+ seconds between segments after a question is usually
someone realizing something. Silence is content.

## Where to start and end

- **Start on the setup line, never the payoff.** If the funny part is at 14:35, the
  clip starts at 14:22 where the premise is established. Viewers need the frame.
- **End 1–2 seconds after the reaction lands.** Do not include the group recovering
  and moving on. The last thing on screen should be the peak.
- **12–35 seconds.** Under 12 has no room for setup. Over 35 needs a second beat to
  justify itself — if it has one, fine; if it doesn't, cut it down.
- **Never cut mid-word.** Check the word timestamps at both ends.

## What to reject

- Anything that needs prior context ("like we talked about last stream")
- Inside jokes with no visible payoff
- Long stretches where nothing happens on screen even if the audio is funny
- Moments whose entire value is a slur, slop, or a pile-on — they get the account
  suppressed and they're not funny
- Anything where the punchline is a name Whisper can't spell and you can't verify

## The honest test

Read your one-line description back. "He insists the door is locked, then it opens
behind him" makes someone want to watch. "Funny moment where they laugh" doesn't —
and if that's the best description you can write, the moment isn't there. Say so
and pick a different one.

## How many

From a 2-hour stream: 4–8 genuinely good clips. From a 20-minute video: 1–3. If you
find fewer, deliver fewer. The number is an output, not a target.
