# The script

Sixty to ninety seconds. Eight to fourteen sentences. Every sentence is one beat and
gets one image, so every sentence has to be picturable.

## The shape

**1. The hook (sentence 1).** State the strangest true fact of the whole story, with
no preamble. Not "today we're looking at" — never that. Not a question.

> "In 79 AD, a Roman city went to sleep and never woke up."
> "The ship was designed to be unsinkable. It sank in two hours and forty minutes."
> "Nine men walked into the reactor building. None of them expected to come back out."

**2. The turn (sentence 2).** Immediately complicate it. The thing everyone assumes
is wrong, or the detail that makes it worse.

> "Vesuvius had been quiet for generations, so nobody read the tremors as a warning."

**3. The build (sentences 3–9).** Chronological. Each sentence advances the clock and
raises the stakes. Concrete nouns and real numbers — "eighteen hours", "four hundred
degrees", "twelve miles" — because numbers are what make a story feel researched.

**4. The pivot (around sentence 10).** The moment it becomes irreversible. This is
where a viewer decides whether to finish, so it should land like a door closing.

**5. The landing (last 2 sentences).** What it left behind, and why we know about it
at all. End on an image, not a lesson. Never end with "and that's the story of."

## Rules that decide whether it holds

- **No throat-clearing.** The first frame is the hook. Anything before it is a
  scroll.
- **One idea per sentence.** Two ideas means one image carries both. It won't.
- **Write for the ear.** Read it aloud. If you stumble, the narrator will too — the
  TTS voice reads exactly what you wrote, including your bad clause.
- **Short sentences accelerate.** As the story tightens, cut sentence length. The
  last four beats should be noticeably shorter than the first four. This is the
  single most effective retention trick in the format and it costs nothing.
- **No rhetorical questions.** "But what really happened that night?" is the sound of
  a channel with nothing to say.
- **Numbers over adjectives.** "Devastating" is filler. "It killed two thousand
  people in eighteen hours" is the same information doing actual work.

## Timing

Edge-TTS at `-8%` runs about 2.4 words per second. So:

| Words | Runtime |
|---|---|
| 130–150 | ~60s (TikTok / Shorts sweet spot) |
| 190–220 | ~90s (upper limit for a short) |
| 400+ | 3 min — a different format; split it into a series |

Count your words before building. If the script runs long, cut a beat rather than
speeding up the voice — a rushed narrator reads as cheap.

## Fact discipline

You will be fact-checked in the comments within an hour of posting, and being wrong
once about a date costs the channel more than ten good episodes earn it.

- Verify every date, casualty figure, and proper name before writing it.
- When sources disagree, say so in the script: "estimates range from two thousand to
  sixteen thousand." That reads as authority, not hedging.
- If you can't verify a detail, cut it. There's always another true detail.
- Never fabricate a quote. Ever. Not even a plausible one.

## Series thinking

The second episode is what builds a channel, not the first. When a subject has more
than one arc in it, write it as a series and end each episode with the hook for the
next one — stated as a fact, not a tease.

> "The man who gave the order was never charged. He was promoted."
