# Running the channel

The kit makes episodes in fifteen minutes. That removes the only real bottleneck,
which means the channel now lives or dies on the decisions below.

## Pick a lane and stay in it

A channel that posts Pompeii, then a shark attack, then a WWII bomber has no
audience — the algorithm can't learn who to show it to, and nobody subscribes to a
feed they can't predict. Pick one and commit for at least 30 episodes:

- **Disasters** — how a specific thing failed. Broad appeal, endless supply.
- **Ancient world** — Rome, Egypt, Bronze Age collapse. Best image availability.
- **Maritime** — shipwrecks and lost expeditions. Strong visual record, loyal niche.
- **Aviation & space** — accident reports are public and read like thrillers.
- **Archaeology** — "they opened the tomb and found…" is a hook generator.
- **Cold cases / vanishings** — huge audience, but check image rights carefully;
  anything involving living or recently-deceased people gets thin on Commons.

The lane also decides your voice. Pick one narrator voice and never change it — it
is the only consistent branding a faceless channel has.

## Titles

Shorts and YouTube rank on title text; TikTok ranks on caption and on-screen text.
Write them separately.

**YouTube title** — a claim, capitalized, 40–60 characters:
- "The City That Vanished In A Single Day"
- "They Opened The Tomb. Then People Started Dying."
- "The Unsinkable Ship Sank In 160 Minutes"

**TikTok caption** — lowercase, unfinished, conversational:
- "they had eighteen hours and nobody left"

Never use the same string for both. A YouTube title in a TikTok caption reads like an
ad; a TikTok caption as a YouTube title doesn't rank.

## Cadence

One episode a day is achievable with this kit and is the right target. Consistency
beats volume — three a day for a week then nothing for two weeks performs worse than
one a day forever. Pick a posting time and hold it.

Batch the work: write five scripts in one sitting, build all five, schedule them out.
Script-writing is the slow part; rendering is not.

## Where to post

Post every episode to all three — it's the same file:

- **YouTube Shorts** — best long-term compounding, real search traffic. Title matters
  most here.
- **TikTok** — fastest early growth, shortest memory.
- **Instagram Reels** — smallest return of the three, but it's a free upload.

Long-form is the actual business: once you have 30 shorts, compile ten related ones
into a 12-minute video with a written intro. Long-form is where watch time and ad
revenue actually live, and you'll have built the library without noticing.

## Monetization, honestly

- **YouTube Partner Program** needs 1,000 subs plus 4,000 watch hours (or 10M Shorts
  views). Shorts alone pay poorly; the compilations are what get you there.
- **The real money in this niche** is a channel that's consistent enough to sell a
  sponsor slot, or a Patreon for early access — not RPM on shorts.
- **Do not** monetize by reuploading other channels' scripts. YouTube's reused-content
  policy is enforced against faceless channels specifically and hardest.

Your script is original, your images are licensed, your voice is synthetic, your
music is synthesized. That combination is fully monetizable — which is exactly why
the discipline in `01-script-formula.md` about not fabricating facts matters. The
only thing that can sink this channel is being wrong.

## The first ten episodes

Don't optimize. Post ten, look at which three did best, and notice what they had in
common — subject, hook shape, length. Then make more of that. Ten episodes at fifteen
minutes each is two and a half hours of work, which is the entire experiment.
