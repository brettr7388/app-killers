# Ordering the tour

The MLS order is not the tour order. MLS photos are uploaded in whatever order the
photographer exported them; a tour has to feel like walking through the house.

## The route

Order the shots the way a buyer would actually move:

1. **Exterior / front** — establishes the house. Always first.
2. **Entry or foyer** — you're inside now.
3. **The main living space** — the room the house is organized around.
4. **Kitchen** — the single most important room in any listing video. Give it two
   shots if you have two good angles.
5. **Dining**, if it's separate and photographs well.
6. **Primary bedroom**, then **primary bath**.
7. **Secondary bedrooms** — one shot each, or skip them entirely if they're plain.
8. **Backyard / pool / view** — the emotional close.
9. **Exterior again**, only if you have a second angle worth ending on.

Two hard rules inside that route:

- **Never cut from an interior back to an exterior mid-tour.** It breaks the walk.
- **Never show a bathroom right after a kitchen.** It's a real estate cliché that
  every agent notices and nobody likes.

## How many shots

Eight to twelve. Under eight feels like a teaser; over twelve and watch time
collapses regardless of how nice the house is.

Cut, in this order, when you have too many: secondary bedrooms, laundry, garage,
hallways, secondary bathrooms, closets. Nobody has ever watched a listing video for
the laundry room.

## Motion

Each shot gets a direction. Vary it or the whole video breathes at one rate and reads
as a slideshow.

| Motion | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `in` | Rooms with a focal point — a fireplace, an island, a view through a window |
| `out` | Reveals. Great for the first exterior and the backyard. |
| `left` / `right` | Wide rooms where the photo is much wider than the frame |
| `up` | High ceilings, staircases, two-story entries |
| `still` | Detail shots — fixtures, finishes, a cover shot with a label |

Rules of thumb: **push in on the hero rooms, pull out on the reveals**, and never use
the same motion three shots in a row. Alternating `in`/`out` on consecutive shots
creates a subtle push-pull rhythm that reads as intentional camera work.

## Timing

- **3.0–3.5s per shot** for vertical/social. Under 2.5s the eye can't take the room in.
- **3.5–4.0s** for the MLS/website cut.
- **Total runtime 30–50 seconds.** A 90-second listing video is not watched to the end
  by anyone, including the agent.
- Give the kitchen and the hero shot an extra half second: `"seconds": 4.0`.

## Choosing which photos

Reject before you order:

- Anything with a person, a pet, or a reflection of the photographer in a mirror
- Dark rooms, blown-out windows, heavy fisheye distortion at the edges
- Visible clutter, trash cans, pet bowls, cords
- Anything with a date stamp or an MLS watermark
- Duplicate angles of the same room — pick the better one, always

Photos with a portrait orientation crop badly to horizontal, and very wide panoramas
crop badly to vertical. Check both if you're building both cuts.

## Labels

Three or four across the entire video, maximum. Put them on the rooms that sell the
house, not on every room. "Chef's kitchen" on the kitchen: yes. "Bedroom 3" on
bedroom 3: never — the viewer can see it's a bedroom.

Good labels state a feature, not a category: `Quartz island & gas range`,
`Primary suite with sitting area`, `Screened lanai`, `No rear neighbors`.

## Language rules that are actually legal rules

Anything on screen or in narration is marketing for a real estate transaction, which
means the Fair Housing Act applies. Describe **the property**, never the people who
might live there or the character of the neighborhood.

| ❌ Never | ✅ Instead |
|---|---|
| "Perfect for families" | "Four bedrooms" |
| "Safe, quiet neighborhood" | "Cul-de-sac lot" |
| "Great schools nearby" | (omit — let the buyer look it up) |
| "Walking distance to church" | "0.4 miles to Main Street" |
| "Ideal for a young couple" | "1,240 sqft, two bedrooms" |
| "Exclusive community" | "Gated community" |

Also never state a fact you weren't given: not square footage, not the year built,
not "recently renovated," not the HOA fee. If the agent didn't provide it, it doesn't
go in the video. A false statement in listing marketing is the agent's liability, and
an agent who gets burned once will never buy from you again.
