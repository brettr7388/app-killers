# Selling these

The video takes ten minutes. Finding the agent takes longer. This is the part of the
business that actually matters, so here it is honestly.

## What to charge

- **$150–$250 per listing video** is the market for a photo-based tour. Agents
  already pay $300–$800 for professional listing photography, so this reads as
  reasonable rather than cheap.
- **$400–$600/month retainer** for unlimited listings is where the real money is —
  and it's the easier sell, because agents hate per-item invoices.
- Charge the same for both orientations. Don't nickel-and-dime a $500/mo client over
  a second render that costs you two minutes.

Do not charge $50. Agents read a $50 video as a $50 video, and you'll spend the same
ten minutes for a client who churns in a month.

## The only pitch that works

Make the video first, for free, unasked.

Pick an agent with an active listing and decent photos. Build the tour. Send it with
no pitch attached:

> Hi [name] — I put together a quick video tour of your listing at [address] using
> the photos from the MLS. No charge, it's yours to post. If it's useful I do these
> for agents around [city] — happy to send details, and if not, keep it anyway.

That's the entire message. No pricing, no deck, no calendar link. The video is the
pitch, and the reason it works is that everyone else sends a paragraph about their
services and nothing to look at.

Two things that matter more than the wording:

1. **Send it to the listing agent, on the channel they actually use.** For most
   agents that's a text or an Instagram DM, not email. Their number is on the sign.
2. **Never send on a weekend.** Agents are working showings. Tuesday through Thursday,
   midday.

## Where to find them

- **Zillow / Realtor.com** — filter to new listings in one zip code. New listings are
  when the agent is actively spending money on marketing.
- **Instagram** — search the city's real estate hashtags. Agents who already post
  video are the best prospects; they've proven they want it and know it works.
- **Local brokerages** — the agent roster page is a lead list. Newer agents respond
  more, established agents pay more.

Stay local at first. "I do these for agents around Orlando" outperforms a generic
pitch by a wide margin, and you can meet them.

## After they say yes

- **Get the photo rights in writing**, in one line: "Confirming you have the right to
  use these listing photos in marketing video." Listing photos usually belong to the
  photographer, licensed to the brokerage. This costs you one sentence and prevents
  the one real legal risk in this business.
- **Ask for their listing feed**, not individual jobs. An agent with six active
  listings is six videos, and they'd rather hand you the whole thing than email you
  every time.
- **Deliver fast.** Same day is a genuine differentiator — the agencies they've used
  before took a week. Your ten-minute turnaround is the actual product.
- **Put their branding on the end card** from day one. Agents care about their name
  on things more than they care about the transitions.

## Scaling it

The bottleneck is never rendering, it's the photos. Ask retainer clients to drop new
listing photos in a shared folder the moment they're shot; then a video is a
ten-minute job you do while the coffee brews, and one person can carry ten agents
without it feeling like work.

## What not to promise

Don't promise views, leads, or offers. You're selling a marketing asset, not results,
and an agent who was promised leads will blame you when the house sits. Sell the
thing: a same-day, professionally-paced tour video for every listing, in both formats,
with their branding on it. That's enough.
